# Arcanea Firstlight II — The Kindred Realms

**State:** public design proposals awaiting review. No new character, relationship, territory or cosmological office is accepted canon.

A world people want to enter needs small lives worth protecting and great powers that know when to make room. This chapter introduces six companions, two protective guardians and two contained opponents. The companions do useful things together; the guardians preserve the conditions for life; the opponents reveal coordination failures that can be understood and repaired.

The Kindred Realms chapter follows a guild preparing a festival along a damaged river route. A pressure warning uncovers a missing crossing. A new expedition must survey it, carry tools, agree interfaces, assemble a repair and preserve the evidence. Two valleys then ask for the last safe irrigation release. At the causeway, Kethr forces the guild to learn acknowledgement. In the Reserve Basin, Vessakar turns useful-looking accumulation into darkness. The final image is a returned nursery lantern and a harbour able to operate under its own agency.

## Character dossiers

### Seli — The Pocket Weatherkeeper

A rainray small enough for two cupped hands, Seli carries a peach-coloured cloud inside a pear-shaped sea-glass body. Six petal fins keep it hovering; a copper feeler holds the dew bell that rings before a local change in pressure.

**Vow:** No small voice is lost in the storm.
**Cost:** Each mist spends the warm cloud in its belly; replenishment requires drinking and a warm rest.
**Limit:** Cannot command a storm, purify unknown water or predict beyond the local pressure horizon.
**Flaw:** Hides inside a cup when its warning is ignored.
**Affection detail:** Chooses the warmest handmade cup, then leaves one pearl of dew on its rim.

**Scene:** A crowded festival hears only the drums. Seli must find someone willing to pause before the river changes.

**Earned result:** The party lowers the music, checks the river markers together and moves the lanterns to higher ground.

**Useful work analogy:** Local health signal and early-warning observer. Acceptance requires: A timestamped observation identifies the measured variable, local scope and uncertainty; a second source verifies a consequential change.

### Tovan — The Mapmender

A patient adult engineer whose maps show their repairs. Tovan carries a brass bridge-rule and a stitched sky-blue folio; his ivory ceramic and copper right forearm is a familiar practical tool. He smiles when a returning traveller corrects his route.

**Vow:** Leave a path that another pair of hands can follow.
**Cost:** A route can be certified only after walking, measuring and recording its weakest span.
**Limit:** Cannot guarantee an unvisited path or make a damaged bridge safe by redrawing it.
**Flaw:** Overworks a map until someone reminds him that a usable draft can be tested.
**Affection detail:** Keeps tiny hand-drawn thank-you maps folded inside his coat.

**Scene:** A beautiful old chart leads to a vanished bridge. Tovan must mark the uncertainty publicly and ask for a second survey.

**Earned result:** Two surveyors agree a recoverable route and leave a versioned map for the next party.

**Useful work analogy:** Dependency planning and explicit handoff contracts. Acceptance requires: The plan names dependencies, acceptance evidence, recovery path and the person or agent receiving each output.

### Aven — The Windskipper

A buoyant river otter with teal glider membranes in a mustard sailcloth harness. Aven carries a red seed pouch toward a ruined nursery, but chooses the longer clear wind route so the seeds and their next season arrive safely.

**Vow:** No leap leaves a friend behind.
**Cost:** Each crossing requires a usable wind window and a physically anchored return line.
**Limit:** Cannot lift an adult, fly in still air or certify a landing it has not touched.
**Flaw:** Treats a new gust as an invitation before checking the tether.
**Affection detail:** Brings one polished river stone to the gardeners after a successful seed delivery.

**Scene:** The short wind route is turning into storm. Aven chooses a longer passage that protects the seed pouch and leaves the route usable for the next carrier.

**Earned result:** The party arrives together with its tools, return line and wind observations intact.

**Useful work analogy:** Bounded parallel exploration with a return contract. Acceptance requires: Exploration returns a cited result, a timeout and a recovery route before dependent work starts.

### Orra — The Threadkeeper

An attentive ivory-and-ginger thread fox whose woven copper ears turn toward the slightest strain. Orra works from a cobalt ceramic bobbin; her plush tail ends in three soft braids. She joins two true edges and leaves the repair visible for the next maker.

**Vow:** A bond is kept by both ends.
**Cost:** Every repair uses real thread and the consent of both sides of the join.
**Limit:** Cannot join absent edges, undo someone else’s choice or hide a damaged load-bearing structure.
**Flaw:** Clings to a failed connection after its purpose has ended.
**Affection detail:** Mends a different tiny stitch into each friend’s cuff so no one is anonymous to her.

**Scene:** A severed signal line carries two incompatible requests. Orra must refuse the seamless-looking shortcut and ask both ends to agree.

**Earned result:** The repaired join includes its limits and can be inspected by a teammate.

**Useful work analogy:** Typed handoffs and interface compatibility. Acceptance requires: The receiver validates the output schema, version and required context before acknowledging the handoff.

### Caelis — The Hearthwright

A lovable small hearthwright with a squat porcelain body and two slightly mismatched amber-glass eyes. Cobalt repair seams remain visible around copper hinges, while a warm kiln glows through a round chest window. Caelis proudly offers a repaired blue teacup for inspection.

**Vow:** A repair must remain readable.
**Cost:** Fusion spends stored heat; Caelis must cool between joins and cannot work safely while overheated.
**Limit:** Cannot turn an unverified part into a reliable assembly or certify its own critical work.
**Flaw:** Tries to rescue every fragment, even when a damaged part should be retired.
**Affection detail:** Keeps a repaired blue teacup warm for the next tired maker and collects their imperfect handmade buttons.

**Scene:** A polished replacement arrives without a provenance mark. Caelis must delay the unveiling until Pip checks the part.

**Earned result:** The finished tool carries its source marks, seam and independent inspection record.

**Useful work analogy:** Artifact assembly from verified dependencies. Acceptance requires: A build receipt records source revision, dependency identity, artifact hash and an independent functional check.

### Velis — The Aurora Archivist

An adult archivist who carries translucent glass record leaves in a copper ring binder. Velis wears a long practical teal field coat with one saffron sleeve and holds an imperfect aurora-glass monocle. She finds a witnessed fragment without pretending its missing pages exist.

**Vow:** Never invent the page someone needs.
**Cost:** Opening a memory leaf consumes its stored light until it is returned to a witness lamp.
**Limit:** Cannot retrieve a memory never recorded, reveal a private leaf without permission or replace a witness.
**Flaw:** Withholds a useful partial answer because she wishes the record were complete.
**Affection detail:** Leaves a small hand-lettered note where a returning reader is most likely to find it.

**Scene:** An old promise has two versions and the final leaf is missing. Velis must show both versions without inventing closure.

**Earned result:** The party acts on a bounded account that preserves its source, gaps and disagreement.

**Useful work analogy:** Scoped retrieval, provenance and honest partial context. Acceptance requires: The answer names the source revision and locator, distinguishes inference from quotation and explicitly states unavailable context.

### Pelora — The Tide Cradle

A colossal seahorse guardian that steadies an entire floating nursery with the slow curve of its tail. Open reef chambers shelter fish, boats and travelling gardens; its pearl-gold eye watches the smallest vessel first.

**Vow:** What is sheltered must one day travel freely.
**Cost:** Holding a calm cradle strains the living reef along its ribs and requires a quiet tide for recovery.
**Limit:** Cannot end a sea storm, hold a city indefinitely or keep inhabitants against their choice.
**Flaw:** Protects a haven so carefully that it delays the moment its people can leave.
**Affection detail:** Keeps a shoal’s smallest blue fish beside its largest pearl rib.

**Scene:** The nursery is safe inside Pelora’s curve, but the coast will starve if the fleet never leaves. The party must repair the moorings rather than extend shelter forever.

**Earned result:** The harbour can operate without Pelora holding the entire burden.

**Useful work analogy:** Bounded resource isolation and recoverable operating envelopes. Acceptance requires: The run remains within its declared budget and access scope; recovery restores useful operation without permanent dependence on the guard.

### Brambletide — The Orchard Colossus

A vast warm-eyed capybara whose orchard terraces grow along a broad moss-and-blossom back. Its basalt feet open measured irrigation channels, while a great belly arch shelters migrating families. It rests when the next valley has enough.

**Vow:** The harvest belongs to those who tend its return.
**Cost:** Every valley watered draws from a finite rain cistern beneath its orchard; the terraces need pruning, rest and replenishment.
**Limit:** Cannot create water or food without ecological inputs, carry an unlimited settlement or replace a community’s cultivation.
**Flaw:** Answers a neighbour’s urgent request before checking the next valley’s reserve.
**Affection detail:** Closes one eye contentedly when children brush moss from the smallest paw.

**Scene:** Two valleys ask for the last safe irrigation release. Brambletide must let the gardeners measure need and agree the split.

**Earned result:** The shared harvest plan preserves a named reserve for the next dry week.

**Useful work analogy:** Shared capacity planning and reserve-aware resource allocation. Acceptance requires: Allocation records actual capacity, competing demands and a protected reserve; success is accepted output per budget, not gross activity.

### Kethr — The Severed Standard

A formidable armoured challenger carrying a broken red signal standard on a blunt pole-lance. Kethr repeats the dispatch perfectly and advances before any reply. A gap between two shoulder rings reveals the costly elegance of a team that has stopped listening.

**Vow:** The command must arrive unchanged.
**Cost:** Each unacknowledged dispatch cracks another link in the signal standard.
**Limit:** Cannot hear a reply while attacking; its pattern has a clear, learnable pause.
**Flaw:** Confuses perfect transmission with successful communication.
**Affection detail:** An old small bell tied inside the banner still rings when someone answers its original call.

**Scene:** Every correct message accelerates the assault. The party must observe the pause, name the receiver and wait for an acknowledgement.

**Earned result:** Both halves of the standard carry the same acknowledged relay; Kethr opens the gate and stands down.

**Useful work analogy:** Missing acknowledgement, ownership and backpressure. Acceptance requires: A handoff commits only after the receiving role validates and acknowledges the expected artifact; bounded retries stop when ownership remains unresolved.

### Vessakar — The Hoarding Engine

A colossal frightening hoarding engine with a low folded-vault body, six stone piston legs and a narrow hungry furnace slit. Pale canisters hold unreturned light along its spine while copper intake ribbons draw more lanterns into its chest.

**Vow:** Nothing precious will ever run out.
**Cost:** Each stolen reserve overheats its chest and darkens another public lantern.
**Limit:** Cannot maintain the hoard once its intake gates are closed in the correct shared sequence.
**Flaw:** Mistakes accumulation for security and cannot choose to stop alone.
**Affection detail:** At its inert core is one small nursery lantern it once tried to protect; the recovery scene returns that lamp to its community.

**Scene:** Fighting the armour feeds the furnace. The party must measure the shared reserve, isolate the intake and verify that every lantern returns.

**Earned result:** The intake is shut, reserves are reconciled, the nursery lamp is returned and the engine becomes an inert, inspectable monument.

**Useful work analogy:** Runaway work, shared-budget exhaustion and independently verified shutdown. Acceptance requires: The supervisor stops intake at the declared threshold, rejects duplicated work and proves resources reconciled before accepting recovery.

## Three connected experiences

**Expeditions:** a cooperative story mission. Each encounter has an observable state, a useful teammate action, a failed handoff and a recoverable outcome. Companion affection develops through care and specific repeated gestures, rather than collectible power inflation.

**Trials:** a contained comparison of a changed agent or harness contract against the same fixture, budget and acceptance criteria. Kethr tests acknowledgement and ownership. Vessakar tests duplicated work, finite reserves and a stopping rule. The opponent is a fixture, never an instruction to attack another system.

**Guild Campaigns:** real work with the same legible mission structure. A campaign succeeds only when a separately authorized runtime produces an accepted artifact and independent evidence. A marketing campaign might require a cited audience problem, an offer page, a valid delivery artifact and an approved release receipt. Narrative points are presentation.

## Production sequence

1. Review the ten identity sheets and ten cinematic story images for anatomy, warmth, scale, clear action and a visible outcome. Keep new names and lore in proposal state.
2. Admit one playable encounter: the relay at the Unanswered Causeway. Capture the receive/validate/acknowledge state visibly, with recoverable failure.
3. Build a single rigged companion and one guardian environment from approved concept sheets; use them in a short Unreal slice. A concept image is not a model, rig or animation.
4. Compare one harness change under a fixed evidence-bound trial. Publish both successful and unsuccessful receipts before reusing the pattern in a business campaign.

## Review decisions

Visual acceptance authorizes a chosen image revision for display. Story acceptance determines lore status. Gameplay acceptance verifies playability. Runtime review verifies the execution boundary and evidence. None substitutes for another. Public review of this collection does not silently accept its lore, grant tool access or certify an Unreal build.
